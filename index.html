Net wealth taxes in Europe

This report compares the net wealth tax systems of the only countries in Europe that still levy such a tax: Norway, Switzerland, Spain, Liechtenstein and Luxembourg. It covers tax object, taxpayer, tax base and valuation, and tax rates. Statutes are cited by section or article.

Comparison at a glance

All three countries tax the net wealth of individuals. Norway has a uniform nationwide system, Spain a national act with strong regional deviations, and Switzerland 26 cantonal systems under a federal framework.

1 Norway

In 2026, Norway levies a net wealth tax for the municipality and the state: a combined 1.0% above NOK 1.9 million and 1.1% above NOK 21.5 million of net wealth (single persons) (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Fortune” section).

1.1 Tax object

  • Principle: All assets with economic value are taxed at market value on 1 January of the assessment year, less debts (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-1(1)).
  • Worldwide principle: Under unlimited tax liability, all domestic and foreign assets are covered (Skatteloven, 1999, § 2-1(9); § 2-2(6)).
  • Short definition by the authority: Net wealth = real estate, bank deposits, shares and business assets after valuation discounts, less debts (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2026a).

Not covered are, among others, conditional rights, temporary rights of use, wage, interest and dividend claims not yet due, copyrights and patent rights held by the author, goodwill, know-how, compulsory pension insurance, and statutory and occupational pension entitlements (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-2(1–2)).

Debts are deductible (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-1). Conditional obligations, interest not yet due and taxes not yet assessed are not deductible (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-3).

1.2 Taxpayer

Liable to tax are individuals and a small group of legal entities. Limited companies (AS/ASA) are expressly exempt.

Exempt are, among others, the royal family, the state, municipalities, universities and certain public banks and funds (Skatteloven, 1999, § 2-30). Non-profit institutions are exempt except for business activities with a turnover above NOK 70,000 (charitable: NOK 140,000) (Skatteloven, 1999, § 2-32(1–2)). Foreign students pay no tax on foreign assets (Skatteloven, 1999, § 2-30(2)).

1.3 Tax base and valuation

Who values: The tax administration (Skatteetaten) sets the values in the pre-filled tax return. Valuation discounts and the reduction of debts are calculated automatically (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2026a; The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2026b). The square-metre rates for dwellings are set annually by the Directorate of Taxes on the basis of calculations by Statistics Norway (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-10(4)).

Procedure: Residential property is valued on a standardised basis (area × price per square metre by type of dwelling, year of construction, size and location) (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-10(4)). For 2026, a new model is used and a price increase of 9.8% (end of 2024 to end of 2026) is assumed (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Important news for 2026” and “Fortune” sections). Taxpayers may claim a lower documented market value (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-10(2), (3), (7)).

Conflict between sources: The act (amended on 23 June 2026, applicable from income year 2026) sets NOK 14 million as the threshold for the 70% valuation (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-10). The authority's publication still states NOK 10 million (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Fortune” section).

Reduction of debts: If a person owns assets with a valuation discount, the deduction for debts is reduced proportionally by the same percentage. The main residence and discounted values count at full value for this purpose. Married couples are taken together (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-19(1–3)). A worked example is provided by The Norwegian Tax Administration (2026b).

Valuation discounts of previous years according to the tax administration:

No discount applies to inventories, receivables, convertible bonds and subscription rights (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2026b).

1.4 Tax rates

Rates 2026 for individuals and estates (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2026a; The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Central tax rates 2026” and “Fortune” sections):

Progression: The tariff is a step tariff with an allowance and two marginal rates (1.0% and 1.1%) (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Fortune” section).

Municipal share: 0.35% is the maximum rate. Municipalities decide on their rate annually within the framework set by the Storting (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Fortune” section). They may not tax individual taxpayers differently (Skatteloven, 1999, § 1-3).

Legal entities: Taxable companies and institutions (not AS/ASA) pay 0.3% to the state; wealth below NOK 10,000 is tax-free (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Fortune” section). No municipal tax is levied (Skatteloven, 1999, § 2-36(2)).

Allowances

Other exceptions and special rules

  • Deferral from 2026: If the net wealth tax exceeds NOK 30,000, it can be deferred for up to three years. The rule targets owners of business assets (shares, partnership interests, operating and commercial property) (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Important news for 2026” section).
  • Valuation discounts act as partial exemptions (main residence 75%, shares 20%, business assets 30%; see Section 1.3).
  • Young people up to 21 years: Lump-sum compensation for personal injury and insurance benefits are exempt from net wealth tax (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-22).
  • Newly established farm: A farm with buildings, equipment and livestock is tax-free in the year of establishment and the following four years (Skatteloven, 1999, § 2-31(1)(d)).
  • Research institutions with basic state funding are exempt for research assets (Skatteloven, 1999, § 2-36(6–7)).
  • Abroad: Debts are reduced proportionally where foreign assets are exempt under a double tax treaty (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-31).
  • Tax limitation for pensioners (income tax): For the income limit, 1.5% of net wealth above NOK 200,000 (spouses NOK 100,000 each) is added; the main residence is disregarded (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Central tax rates 2026” and “Special deductions, tax credits and tax limitations for 2026” sections).

2 Switzerland

In Switzerland, all cantons must levy a wealth tax on individuals and a capital tax on legal entities; each canton determines its own tariffs, tax rates and allowances (StHG, 1990, Art. 1(3), Art. 2(1)). The Confederation itself does not levy a wealth tax; the StHG only sets the framework.

2.1 Tax object (federal framework)

  • Total net wealth: The wealth tax applies to total net wealth (StHG, 1990, Art. 13(1)). All cantons adopt this principle (e.g., StG ZH, 1997, § 38; StG BE, 2000, Art. 46; StG LU, 1999, § 43).
  • Usufruct: Assets subject to usufruct are attributed to the usufructuary (StHG, 1990, Art. 13(2)).
  • Real estate funds: For collective investment schemes with direct real estate ownership, only the difference between total assets and real estate is taxable (StHG, 1990, Art. 13(3)).
  • Exempt: Household effects and personal items (StHG, 1990, Art. 13(4)). Several cantons additionally exempt claims to periodic benefits and tied pension provision (e.g., StG BE, 2000, Art. 47; StG SG, 1998, Art. 63; StG TG, 1992, § 42; LCdir NE, 2000, Art. 51; LIPP GE, 2009, Art. 55).
  • Types covered (example of Neuchâtel, applies accordingly everywhere): real estate, securities, receivables, cash and accounts, operating capital, machinery and inventories, art, jewellery, vehicles, life insurance, rights of use (LCdir NE, 2000, Art. 47).
  • Debts are deductible, guarantees only if called upon (e.g., StG ZH, 1997, § 46; StG BE, 2000, Art. 62).

2.2 Taxpayer (federal framework)

The wealth tax applies only to individuals. Legal entities instead pay a capital tax on their equity (StHG, 1990, Art. 2(1); Art. 29).

The cantons may credit the profit tax against the capital tax and reduce the tax on participations (StHG, 1990, Art. 29(3), Art. 30(2)).

2.3 Tax base and valuation (federal framework)

Basic rules of the StHG:

Who values: The taxpayer declares; the cantonal assessment authority reviews and assesses (StHG, 1990, Art. 46). In most cantons, real estate is officially appraised:

Securities: Stock exchange price at year-end; unlisted securities are appraised by earning value and net asset value (e.g., StG BE, 2000, Art. 49; StG UR, 2010, Art. 50; StG TG, 1992, § 46). Life insurance: surrender value (e.g., StG ZH, 1997, § 45; StG LU, 1999, § 46).

2.4 Tax rates of the 26 cantons

The cantons use three types of tariff: proportional (e.g. TG 1.1‰), progressive in brackets (e.g. ZH 0–3‰) and brackets with a uniform rate above a threshold (e.g. SH, GR, NE, SO). In most cantons, the statutory rate is a “simple tax” that is multiplied by the cantonal and communal tax multipliers (e.g., StG ZH, 1997, § 2; § 188; StG GL, 2000, Art. 2; StG OW, 1994, Art. 67; StG LU, 1999, § 60). The rates below are therefore base rates; the actual burden depends on the tax multiplier, which is not set in the act. Section 2.5 shows the effective burden in 2026.

2.5 Effective burden in the cantonal capital (2026)

With net wealth of CHF 1 million, a single person in the cantonal capital pays between CHF 917 (Zug, 0.92‰) and CHF 6,804 (Neuchâtel, 6.80‰) in wealth tax to the canton and municipality in 2026. With CHF 10 million, the range extends from 1.24‰ (Stans) to 8.02‰ (Genève) (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, 2026c).

Method. The calculation has three steps: deduct allowances, apply the tariff, and multiply the result by the sum of the tax multipliers of the canton and the cantonal capital. Church tax and income-dependent caps are not included. A gross salary of CHF 100,000 was assumed for the ESTV calculator.

Why the tax multipliers are added. The cantonal act only sets the simple tax, i.e. the tariff at 100% (StG ZH, 1997, § 2(1)). Canton and municipality then each decide on a tax multiplier as a percentage of this simple tax; in Zurich, the cantonal parliament for two years and the municipalities annually (StG ZH, 1997, § 2(2); § 188). Both multipliers apply to the same simple tax. They can therefore be added and the total tax calculated in one step; this is the column “Total” in Table 4.

Total tax = simple tax × (multiplier of the canton + multiplier of the municipality) / 100

Effective burden in per mille = total tax / net wealth × 1,000

  2. Simple tax under the basic tariff (StG ZH, 1997, § 47(1)):

If the sum of the multipliers in Zurich were 300% instead of 214%, the tax would be 935.50 × 3.00 = CHF 2,806.50, i.e. 2.81‰. Exceptions to simple addition:

  • Geneva: Only the basic tax is multiplied by 147.5% + 45.49%. The additional tax is added without surcharges (LIPP GE, 2009, Art. 59).
  • Basel-Stadt: The tariff in § 50 is already the full tax. The ESTV splits it half each between canton and city; the total of 100% therefore changes nothing (StG BS, 2000, § 50; Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, 2026a).
  • Uri: The act sets a separate simple tax of 1.0‰ each for the canton and the municipality (StG UR, 2010, Art. 57). Because both are equal, addition leads to the same result.
  • Church tax: Would be a third multiplier on the same simple tax; it is deliberately not included here.

Verification. All 208 cases (26 cantons × 8 model cases) were compared with the ESTV tax calculator; 189 deviate by less than 1% (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, 2026c). The remaining 19 have two causes:

  • Inflation adjustment (GE, GR, JU, VD, ZG): For 2026, the ESTV calculates with adjusted brackets and deductions that are not yet included in the statutory PDFs, e.g. ZG first bracket CHF 254,000 instead of 250,000, GR CHF 76,300 instead of 70,000, JU exemption limit CHF 58,000 instead of 55,000, GE first bracket CHF 118,723 instead of 111,059 (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, 2026b; cf. StG ZG, 2000, § 44; StG GR, 1986, Art. 64; LI JU, 1988, Art. 48; LIPP GE, 2009, Art. 59).
  • Basel-Stadt, married couple with CHF 500,000: income-dependent reduction of 25% for low taxable income (StG BS, 2000, § 51).

Tables 5a and 5b therefore show the values of the ESTV calculator with the 2026 parameters. For Schwyz, the ESTV tariff data for 2026 confirm the rate (0.6‰) and deductions of the 2019 act (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, 2026b; StG SZ, 2000, §§ 47–48).

The range widens with wealth: where a canton has high allowances or a zero bracket (ZG, ZH), the burden rises sharply between CHF 1 and 10 million; with proportional tariffs (LU, NW, OW, SZ), it remains almost unchanged.

For married couples with children, the burden falls mainly for small fortunes, because the allowances have a strong effect there; from CHF 5 million, both households are almost on a par.

3 Spain

Spain taxes the net wealth of individuals with a national step tariff of 0.2% to 3.5% above an allowance of EUR 700,000; the Autonomous Communities may regulate the allowance, the tariff and reductions themselves (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 28, 30; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 13).

The tax was de facto suspended in 2008 by a 100% reduction, reintroduced in 2011 and retained indefinitely from 2021 (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 12). It does not apply in the Basque Country and Navarre (own foral regimes) (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 2; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 12). The revenue is fully assigned to the Autonomous Communities (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 13).

3.1 Tax object

  • Principle: The tax is levied on net wealth = all assets and rights with economic content less charges, debts and personal obligations (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 1; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 12).
  • Valuation date: 31 December; the wealth held on that day is decisive (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 29; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 14).
  • Presumption: Assets held on the last valuation date are deemed to still be held unless their transfer or loss is proven (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 3).

Exempt assets (Art. 4 LIP) (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 4; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, pp. 18–32):

Debts: Proven debts are deductible at nominal value on 31 December; interest never (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 25; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 54). Guarantees and debts relating to exempt assets are not deductible; in the case of partial exemption (e.g. main residence above EUR 300,000) proportionally (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 25; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 55).

3.2 Taxpayer

Liable to tax are exclusively individuals; legal entities are not taxpayers (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 1; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 12).

Filing obligation: If tax is payable or gross wealth exceeds EUR 2,000,000 (even if the tax is zero) (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 37; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 7).

3.3 Tax base and valuation

Who values: The tax is self-assessed; taxpayers value their assets according to the statutory rules and file form 714 electronically (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 36; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 8). The base is generally determined directly, exceptionally estimated (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 26). The cadastral value and the reference value are set by the Directorate-General for the Cadastre (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 39). A contradictory expert appraisal is only possible for jewellery, vehicles, art and other rights (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 27).

Procedure: Scheme = gross wealth − debts = tax base; − allowance = taxable base; × tariff = full tax; − cap, foreign tax, Ceuta/Melilla, regional deductions and reductions = amount payable (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 35).

Cap and minimum tax: Net wealth tax plus personal income tax may not exceed 60% of the personal income tax bases. The reduction is limited to 80% of the net wealth tax; at least 20% is always payable (Ley 19/1991, 1991, Art. 31; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, pp. 69, 72). Non-income-producing assets (art, jewellery, boats, undeveloped land) are not counted for the cap (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 71). Since the Supreme Court rulings of October/November 2025, the cap also applies to non-residents (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, pp. 5, 69).

Reductions and deductions 2025

3.5 Temporary solidarity tax on large fortunes (ITSGF) and transitional rules

Since tax year 2022, the Spanish state has levied a solidarity tax on large fortunes (Impuesto Temporal de Solidaridad de las Grandes Fortunas, ITSGF) alongside the net wealth tax. It applies to net wealth above EUR 3 million and is credited against the net wealth tax. Above all, it prevents regions from reducing their net wealth tax on large fortunes to zero (Ley 38/2022, 2022, Preámbulo V; Art. 3.Uno).

Purpose and legal basis. The tax was introduced by Ley 38/2022 of 27 December 2022 and has applied since 29 December 2022 (Ley 38/2022, 2022, Art. 3.Nueve, note). The legislator states two aims: revenue in times of energy crisis and inflation, and harmonisation between the regions. Persons living in a region that has fully or partly abolished the net wealth tax should not pay substantially less than in other regions (Ley 38/2022, 2022, Preámbulo V).

Structure compared with the net wealth tax:

ITSGF tariff (on the base after deduction of EUR 700,000) (Ley 38/2022, 2022, Art. 3.Once):

The rates from the second bracket correspond to the upper brackets of the national net wealth tax tariff (Section 3.4). Because the base is reduced by EUR 700,000, the ITSGF is actually payable only from net wealth of around EUR 3.7 million.

Credit instead of double taxation. The net wealth tax actually paid is deducted from the ITSGF (Ley 38/2022, 2022, Art. 3.Quince). Anyone who already pays at least as much net wealth tax in their region owes no ITSGF. Only wealth that is not taxed, or hardly taxed, by its region is affected (Ley 38/2022, 2022, Preámbulo V).

Time limit and extension. Originally, the tax was to apply only in the first two tax years (2022 and 2023), followed by an evaluation (Ley 38/2022, 2022, Art. 3.Veintitrés, Veintiocho). Real Decreto-ley 8/2023 extended it until wealth taxation has been reviewed as part of the reform of regional financing (Ley 38/2022, 2022, note to Art. 3; Agencia Tributaria, 2025, p. 61). There is therefore currently no end date.

4 Liechtenstein

Liechtenstein does not levy a separate net wealth tax with its own tariff. Instead, a notional return of 4% of net wealth (“Sollertrag”) is added to income and taxed under the income tax tariff; wealth tax and income tax thus form a single tax (SteG, 2010, Art. 5; Art. 14(2)(l); Art. 19).

4.1 Tax object

  • Principle: All movable and immovable assets; assets of partnerships are attributed to the partners (SteG, 2010, Art. 9(1–2)).
  • Worldwide principle with exceptions: Taxpayers with unlimited liability are taxed on all their assets (SteG, 2010, Art. 6(1)), but foreign real estate and permanent establishments are exempt (SteG, 2010, Art. 10(e–f)).
  • Further exemptions: Household effects, personal items and private vehicles up to CHF 26,400 (married couples CHF 52,800), professional tools up to CHF 2,100, publicly accessible collections, agricultural stocks (SteG, 2010, Art. 10(a–d)).
  • Debts are deductible (SteG, 2010, Art. 11).

4.2 Taxpayer

4.3 Tax base and valuation

  • Valuation date and standard: Market value at the beginning of the tax year (SteG, 2010, Art. 12(1)).
  • Securities: Market price; unlisted securities and receivables at market value, as a rule not below nominal value (SteG, 2010, Art. 12(1)(c–d)).
  • Life insurance: Surrender value (SteG, 2010, Art. 12(1)(f)).
  • Real estate: Earning value, at least the tax appraisal value (SteG, 2010, Art. 12(2)).
  • Business assets: Acquisition or production cost less depreciation (SteG, 2010, Art. 12(3)).
  • Conversion into income: 4% of taxable wealth is deemed income; the government reviews the rate every four years (SteG, 2010, Art. 5; Art. 14(2)(l)).

4.4 Tax rates

The notional return is taxed together with other income under the national tax tariff. For single persons (SteG, 2010, Art. 19(1)(a)):

Wider brackets apply to single parents and married couples; the joint basic allowance for married couples is CHF 31,710 (SteG, 2010, Art. 19(1)(b–c)). The brackets are adjusted once inflation reaches 3% (SteG, 2010, Art. 20).

Allocation between the state and municipalities: The municipality of residence levies a municipal surcharge on the national tax, which the municipal council sets annually at between 150% and 250% of the national tax (SteG, 2010, Art. 75).

Effective burden (own calculation): The marginal rate of the national tax is at most 8%; relative to wealth, this is 8% × 4% = 0.32%. With a municipal surcharge of 150–250%, the marginal burden on wealth is 0.80–1.12% (SteG, 2010, Art. 5; Art. 19; Art. 75).

Worked example (own calculation, without other income): Single person, net wealth CHF 1 million. Notional return = 4% × 1,000,000 = CHF 40,000. National tax = 0.03 × 40,000 − 581 = CHF 619. With an assumed municipal surcharge of 200%, CHF 1,238 is added; in total CHF 1,857 or 0.19% of wealth. With further income, the burden rises because the notional return then falls into higher brackets.

5 Luxembourg

Since 2006, Luxembourg has taxed only the wealth of corporations; since 2016, the tax has been 5‰ up to EUR 500 million and 0.5‰ above that, but at least EUR 535 to 4,815 (Guichet.lu, 2026; Administration des contributions directes, 2025). The legal basis is the Net Wealth Tax Act of 16 October 1934. It was introduced in Luxembourg by the ordinance of 31 December 1940, applied from 1941 and provisionally retained after the war (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, fn. 1).

Note on the version of the act: The version of the Net Wealth Tax Act used reproduces the original wording. It states amounts in francs and Reichsmark and still contains the rules for individuals (allowances, household taxation) (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, §§ 5, 11). Later amendments, above all the abolition for individuals in 2006, are only stated on the authority's website (Guichet.lu, 2026). Where the two differ, the authority's website describes current practice. The rate and the minimum tax were additionally verified online with the tax administration (as at 4 October 2026); there, the current status differs from the original text (Administration des contributions directes, 2025).

5.1 Tax object

  • Total wealth: Taxpayers with unlimited liability are taxed on their total wealth, taxpayers with limited liability only on their domestic wealth (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 1(2), § 2(2)).
  • Current practice: The company's net business assets according to the balance sheet, i.e. fixed assets, inventories, intangible assets, securities and receivables (Guichet.lu, 2026).
  • Worldwide principle with restriction: Foreign assets of resident companies are included, but are generally exempt in states with which a double tax treaty exists (Guichet.lu, 2026).
  • Participations: Shares in qualifying subsidiaries of 10% or more or with an acquisition price of EUR 1.2 million or more are exempt (Guichet.lu, 2026).

5.2 Taxpayer

The Minister of Finance may extend the group of taxpayers and exempt cooperatives (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, §§ 21–22).

5.3 Tax base and valuation

  • Standard: The value determined under the Valuation Act (§§ 73–77), rounded down to full 10,000 francs (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 4).
  • Who values: The tax administration (ACD) sets a unit value for each economic unit by means of an assessment notice; the standard is the estimated market value (Guichet.lu, 2026). Real estate is valued under the real property tax procedure, other assets at their real value on 1 January (Guichet.lu, 2026).
  • Minimum wealth: The original text taxes corporations on at least 500,000 francs (public limited companies) or 200,000 francs (private limited companies) (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, §§ 6(1), (7) no. 1(b)). This rule is obsolete; today a minimum tax applies instead (see Section 5.4) (Administration des contributions directes, 2025).
  • Assessment cycle: Main assessment for three years based on the value at the beginning of the preceding calendar year (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 12). Reassessment in case of a large change in value; the act states more than one fifth or 2 million francs, the authority today 20% or EUR 75,000 (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 13; Guichet.lu, 2026). Subsequent assessment when tax liability begins (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 14).

5.4 Tax rates

  • Rate (since 2016): 5‰ on taxable wealth up to EUR 500 million; above that EUR 2.5 million plus 0.5‰ of the excess (Administration des contributions directes, 2025). The original text still contains the original uniform rate of 5‰ (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 8); in 1941, only 1‰ was levied on a transitional basis (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, fn. 1 c). Calculation = tax base × tax rate (Guichet.lu, 2026).
  • Minimum tax (§ 8(2) VStG): From tax year 2025, resident companies pay at least EUR 535 (balance sheet total up to EUR 350,000), EUR 1,605 (up to EUR 2 million) or EUR 4,815 (above). From 2017 to 2024, there were seven brackets up to EUR 32,100 (Administration des contributions directes, 2025). The simplification is based on the Act of 20 December 2024 and follows a ruling of the Constitutional Court of 2023 (Administration des contributions directes, 2024; Deloitte Luxembourg, 2024). The minimum tax is reduced by the corporate income tax of the previous year (Administration des contributions directes, 2025).
  • No allowance for companies; the minimum tax takes its place. The allowances for individuals (100,000 francs each for the taxpayer, wife and children) have been irrelevant since 2006 (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 5; Guichet.lu, 2026).
  • Reduction through a reserve: The tax is reduced if the company allocates five times the reduction to a reserve and maintains it for five years; limited to the corporate income tax and employment fund contribution of the same year (Guichet.lu, 2026).
  • Lump-sum taxation: If wealth is shifted through foreign relationships, the director of taxation may set a lump sum; for taxpayers with limited liability, the administration may waive the tax or set a lump sum for economic reasons (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, §§ 9–10).
  • Payment (according to the original text): In four instalments on 10 May, 10 August, 10 November and 10 February (Vermögensteuergesetz 1934, 1934, § 16).
  • Allocation between state and municipalities: Only the state tax administration is responsible; neither the act nor the authority's website mentions a municipal share (Guichet.lu, 2026).

Notes on sources and open points

  • Norway – threshold for the main residence: Act NOK 14 million (Skatteloven, 1999, § 4-10(2)), authority's publication NOK 10 million (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2025, “Fortune” section). The amendment to the act of 23 June 2026 is more recent.
  • Norway – valuation discounts: The tax administration's table only extends to 2025 (The Norwegian Tax Administration, 2026b); for 2026, the statutory percentages apply (Skatteloven, 1999, §§ 4-10–4-40).
  • Spain – Andalusia: The general allowance is missing in the handbook; only the values for persons with disabilities are printed (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, pp. 57–58).
  • Spain – Murcia: Changed with effect from 1 January 2025 according to the handbook, but the table shows the national tariff (Agencia Tributaria, 2025, pp. 5, 66).
  • Spain – version of the act: The statutory file reflects the version of 2023, the handbook tax year 2025. Where the two might differ, the handbook is cited. The English version has identical content and is not cited separately.
  • Spain – ITSGF: Now supported by the statutory text (Section 3.5). It remains open when the reform of regional financing will come and the extension will end.
  • Switzerland – tax multipliers: The tax multipliers are not set in the acts; they are taken from the ESTV data for 2026 (Section 2.5). The burden calculation includes neither church tax nor income-dependent caps; in GE, GR, JU, VD and ZG, the amounts for 2026 differ from the statutory PDFs due to inflation adjustment.
  • Switzerland – Schwyz: Version of 2019; according to the ESTV tariff data for 2026, rate and allowances are unchanged (Eidgenössische Steuerverwaltung, 2026b).
  • Switzerland – capital tax: The capital tax on legal entities is only described within the federal framework, not for each canton.
  • Luxembourg – tax rate: The version of the act used contains the original wording. The current tariff (5‰ up to EUR 500 million, 0.5‰ above) and the minimum tax are documented online by the tax administration (Administration des contributions directes, 2025). The consolidated version of the act (texte coordonné) from Legilux would nevertheless be a better primary source.
  • Liechtenstein – municipal surcharge: The act only states the range of 150–250% (SteG, 2010, Art. 75); the rate of Vaduz is not documented; 200% is assumed in the worked example.
